Warm-up · 5 min

Green Light, Go!

Setup: Pick a start line and a "finish" cone about 20 yards away — a driveway, sidewalk stretch, or open grass all work. No ball for this one. Dad is the traffic caller.

How it works: Everybody moves toward the finish on Dad's calls: "green light" = jog, "turbo green" = quick run, "yellow" = slow-motion skip or high-knees, and "red light" = freeze instantly. Mix the calls up so it stays a game. Reach the finish, then jog back and swap so Son gets to be the caller too. Great way to wake the legs up and get some easy movement in.

Cues: On "red" freeze like a statue — quick brakes are great for young athletes. Land softly, stay light on your feet, and keep your head up to hear the next call.

🎉 Make it fun: Anyone who moves on a "red light" has to do a goofy celebration dance before restarting. Let Son sneak in a surprise "turbo green!" to catch Dad flat-footed.

Jog, skip and sprint toward the finish on Dad's calls — freeze on "red light," then swap callers. A no-ball wake-up for the legs.

Warm-up · 5 min

Sprinter's Spark Form Drills

Setup: Two cones about 15 yards apart make a there-and-back lane. No ball — this is all about teaching the legs to run smooth and springy. Do each drill up to the far cone, then easy-jog back and start the next one.

How it works: Roll through these one at a time: ankling (tiny quick steps up on the balls of your feet), high-knee marches that turn into high-knee skips, heel-ups (butt-kicks, heels flicking up behind), a relaxed backward jog, and finish with 2 build-up strides — start easy and smoothly accelerate to about 80% by the far cone. Copy each other and keep it light and bouncy.

Cues: Run tall like a string is pulling the top of your head up, arms swinging front-to-back (not across the body), quick soft contacts with the ground. These little drills are how runners get faster and stay healthy.

🎉 Make it fun: Take turns being the "coach" who demos each drill — the other has to mirror it exactly. The build-up strides get a "3-2-1 launch!" countdown so they feel like a rocket taking off.

Ankling, high-knee marches and skips, heel-ups, a backward jog, and two build-up strides — the toolkit that builds smooth, springy running.

Endurance · ~20 min

Explorer Run-Walk Intervals

Setup: Pick a safe out-and-back route or a loop you can see — sidewalk, park path, or around the yard/block. Nothing to carry but water. This is the heart of today: a gentle base-builder for a 10-year-old, so we keep it conversational the whole way.

How it works: Run and walk in easy chunks — 5 rounds of (2½ minutes easy jog + 1½ minutes walk). That's about 12–13 minutes of actual running your very first time out, spread out so it never feels like a slog. Run side by side and chat the whole time; if you can't talk in full sentences, you're going too fast — slow it down. Walk breaks are part of the plan, not a "failure," so enjoy them. Since this is running session #1, this is your baseline — every future run week we'll nudge it up just a touch.

Cues: Easy, relaxed shoulders, soft steps, breathe naturally. "Talk test" the whole way. Sip water on the walk breaks. If anything pinches or hurts, stop and walk it out — no toughing it out.

🎉 Make it fun: Play "Explorer" — during each jog, pick a landmark up ahead (a mailbox, a tree, a lamppost) and "claim" it as you pass. Count how many you claim by the end and give them silly names. Talk about your favorite part of the day while you run.

Five easy rounds of jog-then-walk — about 12–13 minutes of running total, side by side and chatting the whole way.

Speed & Fun · ~10 min

Rocket Reaction Dashes & Freeze-Chase

Setup: Two cones about 20 yards apart. Start together at the first cone. No ball. This is the fast, giggly part after the steady run.

How it works: Two mini-games. (1) Rocket Reaction Dashes: both crouch at the line and go the instant Dad drops a raised hand (or shouts "GO!") — sprint to the far cone. Time Son's 20-yard dash a couple of times and try to beat it. Give Dad a little head-start handicap so it's a real photo finish. (2) Freeze-Chase: one person jogs around the space, the other chases; when the chaser yells "FREEZE!" both stop, then swap roles. Do 4–6 short dashes and a couple rounds of chase — walk to recover between sprints.

Cues: React fast on the "go," drive your arms hard, lean forward out of the start, and run through the finish cone (don't slow down early). Full effort for a few seconds, then rest — that's how speed is built.

🎉 Make it fun: Fake-out starts — Dad raises his hand halfway and pulls it back; anyone who jumps early does a silly "false-start wiggle." Winner of each dash gets to invent the next start signal (clap, whistle, "banana!").

Explode on the surprise "go," sprint the 20 yards, and race Dad off a handicap — quick bursts with full recovery between.

Cooldown · ~10 min

Walk, Stretch & Chat

Walk it off: Easy walking together until your breathing fully settles and your heart rate comes down — a few minutes, no rush. Sip water the whole time.

Stretch: Give the running muscles some love — calves, quads, hamstrings, hips, and a gentle glute/figure-4 stretch. Hold each about 20 seconds, nice and relaxed, no bouncing.

Recap one great thing: On the walk, each of you names one awesome thing the other did — a strong finish through the cone, never quitting on a jog, a lightning reaction start. First run in the books deserves a big high-five and a proud "we did it." Then log it below so it counts! 🎉

🌧️ Rain Backup (small-space, ~45 min)

Storm rolls in or the heat's just too much? No running room needed — a couple of square feet each is plenty. We keep the running "engine" working in place and add a bodyweight circuit.

In-place cardio ladder (2 rounds, 40 sec each): marching high-knees → jogging in place → fast feet → standing mountain climbers → heel-ups (butt-kicks) in place. Count your fast-feet touches in the last 20 seconds and try to beat it next round.

Form drills in place: Ankling, arm-swing practice (tall posture, front-to-back), and slow-motion high-knee marches for 40 seconds — same smooth mechanics we build outside, just standing still.

Strength & hops circuit (2 rounds): 30 seconds each — squats · push-ups (knees OK) · plank hold · little two-foot jumps · calf raises. Rest a minute between rounds.

Finish: The normal stretch — calves, quads, hamstrings, hips, ~20 seconds each — then trade one "great thing" and a high-five.

🎉 Make it fun: Reaction game with no running room — Dad claps and you have to switch moves instantly (clap once = fast feet, clap twice = freeze). Miss a call and you owe five extra calf raises. 😄
