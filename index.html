Warm-up 1 · 0–5 min

Shadow Dribble Follow-the-Leader

Setup: One soccer ball, open grass or driveway ~15 yards long. No markers needed — you'll wander freely.

How it works: Son leads, gently dribbling the ball wherever he wants — curves, figure-eights, slow then quick. Dad is the "shadow" jogging 2–3 steps behind, copying every turn and change of speed. Swap leader every ~90 seconds so both of you lead and follow.

  • Small touches — keep the ball within one step at all times.
  • Leader: mix it up — sharp turns, little bursts, then slow it down.
  • Loosen up as you go: roll the shoulders, swing the arms.
Make it fun: Leader can throw in a silly "signature move" (a spin, a hop, a robot walk) and the shadow HAS to copy it. First one to laugh does a victory lap. 🤖
Warm-up 2 · 5–10 min

Green Light Form Freeze

Setup: Mark a start line and a "finish" line ~20 yards apart with 2 cones (or chalk). You'll travel back and forth. This is a stop/start reaction game that also wakes up your running form.

How it works: Dad is the caller. On "Green light!" both of you move forward doing the called form drill; on "Red light!" freeze instantly and hold your balance. Cycle the drills each green: high knees → butt kicks → skips → fast feet. Race to the far cone, then turn around and come back. Let the son be the caller for the return trip.

  • High knees: knee to hip height, quick ground contact, tall posture.
  • Butt kicks: heels flick up toward your seat, stay light.
  • Skips: drive the knee up and "reach" for height, not speed.
  • On "Red light!" freeze — whoever wobbles first does 3 star jumps.
Make it fun: The caller can fake you out — say "Grrreeeee… RED!" Whoever gets tricked into moving has to do their goofiest freeze pose. 🕺
Main Run · 10–33 min

Explorer Run/Walk Intervals

Setup: Pick a safe out-and-back route or a loop with easy landmarks (a tree, a mailbox, a lamppost, a corner). No pace pressure — this is your very first running session, so we keep it easy and playful to build a base.

How it works: Start with a 2-minute easy jog to warm in. Then run this pattern 6 times: jog easy for 2 minutes to the next landmark, then walk 1 minute to recover. Finish with a 1–2 minute easy walk/jog. That's about 14 minutes of running spread over ~23 minutes — perfect for round one.

  • Conversation test: jog slow enough that you can still chat in full sentences. If you can't talk, slow down.
  • Land soft and quiet — "ninja feet," short steps.
  • Use the walk minute fully — shake out the arms, take a sip of water.
  • Effort by feel, not speed. Slower is totally fine today.
Make it fun: Turn it into a quest — each landmark is a "checkpoint" to unlock. Take turns naming what you're running toward ("to the red mailbox!"), and give each checkpoint a code name. Six checkpoints = mission complete. 🗺️
Fun Speed · 33–43 min

Rocket Strides & Handicap Chase Races

Setup: Mark a ~30-yard straight lane with 2 cones (start + finish). Flat grass is easiest on the legs.

How it works: Two mini-games back to back. Rocket Strides (x4): from the start cone, gradually build up speed over 30 yards — start easy, "take off" to about 80% by the finish (not an all-out sprint), then walk back to recover. Handicap Chase Races (x4–5): Son starts a few yards ahead; Dad counts "3-2-1-GO!" and tries to catch him before the finish. Adjust the head-start so races stay close and exciting.

  • Strides: smooth acceleration, tall and relaxed — "float, don't strain."
  • Pump the arms front-to-back, hands relaxed (not across the body).
  • Full walk-back recovery between every rep — quality over quantity.
  • Chase races: keep the head start fair so it comes down to the line.
Make it fun: Countdown like a rocket launch — "3… 2… 1… blast off!" Winner of each chase race picks the celebration; if Dad wins, Son gets an extra half-yard head start next round. 🚀
Cooldown · 43–48 min

Easy Walk, Light Stretch & Chat

How it works: Walk together for 2–3 easy minutes until your breathing settles. Then do a few gentle, held stretches (no bouncing): calves, quads, hamstrings, and hip flexors — about 15–20 seconds each side. Keep it relaxed and chatty.

  • Calf: hands on a wall, back leg straight, heel down.
  • Quad: stand tall, pull one heel toward your seat (hold something for balance).
  • Hamstring: one heel out, hinge forward with a flat back.
  • Breathe slow and easy — this is the wind-down.
Recap & high-five: Tell your son one specific thing he did great today — "your ninja-quiet feet on the jog were awesome." Then let him name his favorite part. End on a fist bump. 🤜🤛
