Warm-up · 5 min

Shadow Tag Sprint

Setup: No ball — this is a reaction-and-chase game to wake up your legs. You just need a bit of open grass. One person is the leader, the other is the shadow, standing about 2 yards behind.

How it works: The leader jogs a slow, winding path — curving left, curving right, speeding up and slowing down — and the shadow copies every move, staying glued 2 yards behind. Then, out of nowhere, the leader yells "GO!" and sprints off; the shadow has 5 seconds to chase and tag them. Jog back, swap roles, and go again. Play for about 5 minutes.

Cues: Stay light and ready on the balls of your feet, watch the leader's hips (not their feet) so you can't be faked out, and explode into your first two steps the instant you hear "GO!"

🎉 Make it fun: The leader gets to add sneaky fake-outs — a shoulder dip or a slow-down right before the real "GO!" Every tag inside 5 seconds is a point; first to 3 points wins the round and picks the next silly leader move. 😄

A no-ball shadow-and-chase warm-up: mirror the leader's winding jog, then react and sprint to tag on the surprise "GO!" Fires up the legs and trains quick reactions and acceleration before the run.

Warm-up · 5 min

Fast-Leg Form Series

Setup: No ball. Mark a lane about 15 yards with a cone at each end. These are the classic running-form drills that teach your legs to be quick, springy and light — the secret to running faster and easier.

How it works: Work down the lane one drill at a time, then walk back and start the next: ankling (tiny quick foot rolls) → A-marches (drive one knee up, tall posture) → A-skips (same, with a skip) → B-skips (knee up, then flick the foot out and paw the ground) → heel flicks → carioca (grapevine side-step) → 2 relaxed build-up strides. One length each, about 5 minutes total.

Cues: Stand tall like a string is pulling the top of your head up, snap the knee up and the foot down quick, and stay bouncy on the balls of your feet. Quality over speed — smooth and springy beats rushed.

🎉 Make it fun: Race the "robot vs. bouncy-ball" challenge — do one length stiff like a robot, then the same drill light and springy like a bouncy ball, and feel the difference. Dad has to guess which one you're doing! 🤖

A no-ball running-form ladder — ankling, A- and B-skips, heel flicks, carioca and build-up strides, one length at a time. Teaches a tall, springy, quick-footed stride that makes running faster and smoother.

Endurance · ~25 min

Fartlek Story Run

Setup: No ball. Find a loop or a stretch of path/grass you can jog along together — a park loop, a quiet sidewalk, or back and forth across the field. This is your first run of the program, so we start gentle: about 12 minutes of actual jogging, broken into four easy "chapters" with a walk break after each. A "fartlek" just means playful speed — we sprinkle in tiny fun surges.

How it works: Run it as 4 chapters, each one 3 minutes of easy jogging + 1 minute of walking. Right in the middle of each jog, Dad calls a story cue — "a friendly dog wants to race!" — and you both do a fun 15-second power surge (a bit faster, not a full sprint) before settling back to the easy jog. That easy-jog pace should be slow enough that you can still chat. Log your longest nonstop jog (it's 3 minutes today) so we can grow it a little each running week.

Cues: "Conversation pace" is the golden rule — if you can't talk, you're going too fast, so ease off. Land softly, breathe in a steady rhythm, and use the walk breaks to fully catch your breath. Finishing feeling good beats finishing fast.

🎉 Make it fun: Take turns being the storyteller — whoever's turn it is invents the surge ("lava on the path!", "catch that ice-cream truck!"). Silliest story of the run wins. Counting the chapters down together makes the last one fly by. 🐉

Four easy 3-minute jogs with walk breaks and playful mid-jog surges — about 12 minutes of gentle running to start building a base. Grows the engine at a fun, kid-friendly conversation pace.

Speed · ~10 min

Relay Shuttle & Catch-Me Races

Setup: No ball. Set two cones about 20 yards apart. Grab one speed stick to use as a relay "baton." This block is the fun, fast part — short bursts with lots of rest, which is exactly how you get quicker.

How it works: Two mini-games. (1) Relay shuttle: sprint from cone to cone and back, hand off the baton, and see how fast you can run a set number of laps as a team. (2) Catch-Me races: line up at one cone and race to the far cone — for the handicap version, Dad gives Son a head start so it's a real photo finish. Mix in one timed solo 20-yard dash for each of you and log the fastest time. Full rest (walk back slowly) between every sprint — these should feel fast, not tiring.

Cues: Explode out low and drive your arms hard, run tall through the finish cone (don't slow up early), and take your time recovering between runs so every sprint is a fast one.

🎉 Make it fun: Make the handicap fair and sneaky — give Son a big enough head start that Dad has to really fly to catch up. Reversed roles, funny running styles ("gorilla sprint!"), and a made-up finish-line commentary make it a blast. 🏁

Short, full-speed shuttle relays plus handicap chase races and a timed 20-yard dash, with big rests between. Builds acceleration, top-end speed and a love of racing — the fun payoff of a running day.

Cooldown · ~10 min

Walk, Stretch & Chat

Walk it off: Easy 3–4 minute walk together until your breathing settles all the way back down. Keep sipping water — it's heading for a hot one.

Stretch: Give the running muscles some love — calves, quads, hamstrings, hip flexors, and glutes. Hold each about 20 seconds, relaxed, no bouncing.

Chat: As you stretch, talk it over — how did the first run feel? Easiest part, hardest part, favorite silly surge story? Each name one great thing from today, big high-five, then tap a button below so it counts. Loved a drill? Tap its ⭐! 🎉

🌧️ Rain Backup (small-space, ~60 min)

Storm rolls in early or you're stuck inside? No field or running room needed — a couple of square feet each is plenty. On a running day we can't jog indoors, so we build the engine and quick feet with an in-place cardio + bodyweight circuit instead.

Warm-up (2 rounds of 90 sec): march in place → jumping jacks → fast feet on the spot (light, quick steps). Rest 30 seconds between rounds.

Cardio + strength circuit (3 rounds, 40 sec on / 20 sec off), all in place: high knees → squats → mountain climbers → push-ups (knees OK) → in-place fast feet → plank hold. Rest a minute between rounds. This keeps the heart working like the run would.

Speed bursts (6 rounds): 10 seconds of all-out high knees or fast feet on the spot, then 30 seconds easy marching. That's your rainy-day "sprints." Time your best 20 seconds of high-knee count if you want a number for the record book.

Finish: The normal stretch — calves, quads, hamstrings, hip flexors, glutes, ~20 seconds each — then trade one "proud moment" and a high-five.

🎉 Make it fun: Reaction round — Dad claps and you switch instantly (one clap = fast feet, two claps = freeze in a plank). Miss a call and you owe five squats. 😄
