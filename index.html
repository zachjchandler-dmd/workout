Warm-up · 5 min

Clock Dribble Reactions

Setup: Set out cones in a big circle like a clock face (about 8–12 yards across) and call them by number — 12 at the top, 3 on the right, 6 at the bottom, 9 on the left (fill in a few in between). You each start in the middle with a ball.

How it works: Dribble around gently in the center. When Dad calls a number — "THREE!" — both of you dribble out to that cone under control, do a quick turn around it, and dribble back to the middle before the next call. Keep the calls coming for about 5 minutes, mixing near and far numbers so you're always reacting and changing direction.

Cues: Little touches with the laces and insides to keep the ball close, head up to find the number fast, and plant-and-turn sharply at the cone. First one back to the middle with the ball still under control gets the point.

🎉 Make it fun: Play "clock says" — Dad can call two numbers in a row ("3… then 9!") or a trick call like "lunchtime = 12!" Anyone who dashes to the wrong number owes a silly clock chime. 🕐⚽

A reaction dribble game on a clock face — hear the number, pick your line, cut out and back with the ball under control. Warms the body up while sharpening close control, awareness, and quick changes of direction.

Warm-up · 5 min

Robot Reboot Mobility

Setup: No ball for this one. Set two cones about 15 yards apart to make a lane. Each move is a "system" the robot powers up — you travel down doing the move, then walk back easy to reboot for the next one.

How it works: Power up each system down the lane: "gears online" = walking knee hugs → "pistons" = walking quad pulls (heel to bum) → "hydraulics" = walking lunge with a torso twist → "side servos" = lateral lunges → "spring test" = ankle bounces / skips → "arm rotors" = big arm circles while marching → "sensor sweep" = leg swings front-to-back at the end cone. About 5 minutes — smooth and controlled, waking up hips, ankles, and legs before the ball work.

Cues: Move with control, not speed. Stand tall, hug the knee in, sink the lunges gently, and keep the core switched on. Breathe easy — this is the "boot-up," not the race.

🎉 Make it fun: Do robot voices and stiff robot arms between moves, then "smooth out" as the system comes online. Dad leads one length, Son the next, and each leader invents one bonus "upgrade" move. 🤖⚙️

A no-ball dynamic-mobility warm-up dressed up as a robot booting its systems. Opens the hips, ankles, and legs and switches the core on, prepping the body for sharp cuts and sprints without any pounding.

Technical · ~7 min

Change-of-Pace Gates

Setup: Build 4 cone "gates" (two cones ~1 yard apart) in a line, about 5 yards between gates. You've got a ball and start at the first gate. Today's skill isn't a move — it's speed control: dribble slow and relaxed, then explode through the gate and slow down again on the other side.

How it works: Dribble toward a gate with small, calm touches — slow… slow… — then, right at the gate, push a bigger touch out in front with your laces and burst through at top speed for 2–3 steps. Settle back down, reset, and do the next gate the same way. It's the "gears": low gear to lull the defender, high gear to blow past. Timed challenge: how many clean change-of-pace gates can you do in 60 seconds? Log it below.

Cues: Really sell the slow — relaxed shoulders, tiny touches — so the burst is a surprise. Push the accelerator touch just far enough to sprint onto it, not so far it runs away. Big difference between the two speeds is the whole point.

🎉 Make it fun: Add engine sounds — a low "rrrrr" idle, then "VROOM!" on the burst. Dad stands loosely past a gate as a "sleepy defender"; if your burst leaves him flat-footed, that gate counts double. 🏎️💨

Change of pace — the skill of lulling a defender at low speed then exploding past. Teaches deliberate speed control and an accelerator touch, one of the simplest ways a young player leaves a defender behind.

Technical · ~7 min

Rebounder Weighted Passing Ladder

Setup: Face the rebounding wall and mark three spots with cones: near (~2 yards), mid (~4 yards), far (~7 yards). Start at the near cone with your ball. This one is about the weight of your pass — how hard you hit it so the return is easy to control.

How it works: From the near cone, pass a soft, gentle pass into the wall, control the rebound, then step to the mid cone and pass a bit firmer, control, then far cone and pass a strong pass, control. Work back down the ladder. The goal is that every rebound comes back at a speed you can settle in one touch. Timed challenge: how many clean weighted passes (pass + controlled first touch) can you make in 60 seconds? Log your best below.

Cues: Open the passing foot and strike through the middle of the ball. Match the power to the distance — near = a whisper, far = a firm drive. Cushion each return out in front where your next pass is easy. Use both feet if you can.

🎉 Make it fun: Call it "Goldilocks passing" — too soft, too hard, or juuust right. Dad shouts a distance at random ("FAR!") and you have to hit that weight on the next pass. Every "just right" in a row builds a combo. 🎯⚽

A wall passing ladder built around pass weight and a clean first touch. Sharpens the feel for how hard to strike a pass over different distances — a habit that makes every touch in a game smoother.

Speed & Agility · ~15 min

Pro-Shuttle Power Circuit

Setup: Line up a quick course (no ball): speed-stick ladder → a 5-10-5 "pro shuttle" (three cones in a line 5 yards apart) → two trapezoid hurdles → a 10-yard sprint finish. Set it so one lap flows straight through.

How it works: Go one at a time and rip through the whole circuit: quick feet through the ladder → into the pro shuttle: start at the middle cone, sprint 5 yards right and touch the line, sprint 10 yards across to the far cone and touch, sprint back 5 to the middle → two-foot hops over the trapezoid hurdles → explode into the 10-yard sprint finish. Walk back to fully recover, then go again. Aim for 5–6 clean laps. Time a lap and log your fastest clean one below.

Cues: Light, quick feet in the ladder (don't look down). In the shuttle, stay low and touch the line with your hand, plant the outside foot hard, and drive back out. Snappy knees over the hurdles, then run through the finish line — don't slow up early. Full rest between laps so every lap is fast.

🎉 Make it fun: Dad runs it too with a fair handicap so it's a real race, and you call out your times. Ring an imaginary bell when someone sets a new fastest lap, and give the shuttle a "5-10-5!" chant on the touches. ⚡🏁

A flowing speed-and-agility circuit built around the 5-10-5 pro shuttle — ladder quickness, hard change-of-direction plants, hurdle spring, and an all-out sprint finish. Builds fast feet, sharp cuts, and acceleration, with a lap time to beat every soccer week.

Game · ~15 min

Nutmeg Cup (1v1)

Setup: Make a box about 12×12 yards with a small goal (2 cones, ~1.5 yards wide) on two opposite ends. One ball, you vs. Dad, 1v1 — you attack one goal, Dad attacks the other.

How it works: Normal 1v1 — dribble through the goal you're attacking (under control) for 1 point. The twist: a clean nutmeg — knocking the ball through your opponent's legs and collecting it on the other side — is worth 2 bonus points any time you pull it off, and a nutmeg-then-goal is the jackpot. After a goal or the ball leaves the box, restart from your own end. Play games to 5 points, or count total points over ~15 minutes. Keep score — this one's for real bragging rights.

Cues: Head up to read Dad's feet — the nutmeg is on when they're apart. Use today's change of pace to set it up: slow him down, then burst or slip it through. Shield the ball when pressured, and on defense keep your feet closer together (no free tunnels!).

🎉 Make it fun: Every nutmeg gets a giant "MEG!" shout and a made-up celebration. Name a "golden minute" where nutmegs are worth triple, and lift an imaginary Nutmeg Cup trophy for the winner. 🏆🥜

A 1v1 game to opposite goals with a nutmeg bonus baked in — decision-making, deception, shielding, and reading a defender under real pressure, in a fast, competitive format that rewards today's change-of-pace work. Keep score for points or games won.

Cooldown · ~5 min

Walk, Stretch & Recap

Walk it off: Easy 2–3 minute walk together, ball tucked under an arm, until your breathing settles. Keep sipping water — a sunny South Georgia morning gets sticky fast.

Stretch: Give the legs some love — calves, quads, hamstrings, hip flexors, and glutes. Roll the ankles out after all that quick footwork. Hold each about 20 seconds, relaxed, no bouncing.

Recap: As you stretch, each name one great thing from today — a burst that blew past Dad, a "just right" pass, a cheeky nutmeg. Big high-five, then tap a button below so your session counts. Loved a drill? Tap its ⭐! 🎉

🌧️ Rain Backup (small-space, ~50 min)

Storms roll in or the grass is soaked? No running room needed — a couple of square feet each is plenty. We keep the ball skills and the sweat going with in-place ball mastery and a bodyweight circuit instead of the field session.

Warm-up (2 rounds of 90 sec): march in place → arm circles & trunk twists → light in-place fast feet. Rest 30 seconds between rounds.

Ball mastery in place (5 min, ball stays under you): toe taps → sole rolls side to side → foundation inside-inside taps → sole-roll pull-backs. 30 seconds each, twice through — quick, controlled touches in one spot.

Strength & quickness circuit (3 rounds, 30 sec on / 30 sec off), all in place: squats → push-ups → in-place fast feet → plank hold → mountain climbers. Rest a minute between rounds.

Finish: The normal stretch — calves, quads, hamstrings, hip flexors, glutes, ~20 seconds each — then trade one "proud moment" and a high-five.

🎉 Make it fun: Reaction round — Dad claps and you switch instantly (one clap = fast feet on the ball, two claps = freeze in a plank). Miss a call and you owe five squats. 😄
