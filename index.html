Father–Son Session

⚽ Soccer Skills Day · ~60 min · Session #1

Gear today: cones · speed sticks · trapezoid hurdles · soccer balls · rebounding wall

First session — keep it light & fun. This is your very first workout together, so treat every time and rep target below as a ceiling, not a goal. Do fewer reps, stop each drill while he's still enjoying it, and let him win a little. The #1 job today is that he finishes wanting to come back Monday.
Optional gear idea (1 per week): a $15–20 pop-up mini goal. It makes King of the Hill and the shooting challenge feel like a real match and gives your son a target to celebrate scoring on. Totally optional — everything today works with cones as goals.

0:00–0:05 · Warm-Up Part 1

Shadow Dribble (Follow the Leader)

Setup: Mark a 15×15 yd box with a cone at each corner. Each of you has your own ball. One is the leader, the other shadows ~2 yds behind. Works perfectly with just two people.

How it works: The leader dribbles freely around the box — changing direction, speed, and throwing in moves (a roll, a cut, a sudden stop) — while the follower copies everything with their own ball, staying a couple yards behind. Swap leader every 45 seconds so you each get to lead.

  • Soft, close touches — the ball stays "on a string."
  • Head up to watch the leader, quick peeks down at the ball.
  • Use both feet and react fast to every change.
Make it fun: The leader gets to be silly — spin moves, tiptoe steps, a sudden freeze, even a little dance over the ball. The follower has to copy the goofiest thing without losing control of their own ball.
0:05–0:10 · Warm-Up Part 2

Mirror Match (reaction / stop-start)

Setup: No ball. Stand ~3 yds apart facing each other inside a 6×6 yd square marked by 4 cones. One of you is the "leader."

How it works: The leader shuffles left, right, forward, back, and freezes at random; the follower mirrors every move like a reflection. Hold each freeze for a beat, then explode into the next direction. Swap leader every 45 seconds.

  • Stay low — bend the knees, weight on the balls of your feet.
  • Quick, choppy steps; don't cross your feet.
  • React on the first flinch, not after.
Make it fun: Add silly freeze poses (flamingo, superhero, statue). If the follower matches the pose within 2 seconds, they steal a point.
0:10–0:15 · Technical Block

Sole-Roll Ball Mastery

Setup: Each of you with your own ball, standing a few yards apart in open space. No cones needed.

How it works: Roll the ball side-to-side under one foot's sole, then switch feet — 20 rolls each. Then "toe taps" (light taps on top of the ball, alternating feet) for 30 seconds, then "roll & push" (sole-roll the ball across your body and receive with the other foot). Do 3 quick rounds.

  • Stay light on the toes — soft touches, ball stays close.
  • Look up between reps to build "head-up" habits.
  • Both feet get equal reps — the weaker foot too.
Make it fun: Race to 20 toe taps — first to finish yells "Messi!" Loser of each round picks the next silly move to add.
0:15–0:20 · Technical Block

Rebounder Wall One-Touch

Setup: Stand ~5 yds from the rebounding wall with one ball. Place a cone where you're standing so you keep the distance honest.

How it works: Pass firmly into the wall with the inside of the foot, then receive the return with one touch and pass straight back — see how many clean one-touches you can string together. Then try alternating feet each pass. Take turns and count your best streak.

  • Strike through the middle of the ball, ankle locked.
  • Open your hips so the returning ball meets a cushioned foot.
  • Small "prep step" before each touch to stay balanced.
Make it fun: Play "don't break the chain" — count one-touches out loud together; a new team-best streak earns a victory handshake you invent.
0:20–0:25 · Technical Block

L-Cut Cone Dribble

Setup: Lay 4 cones in an "L": three cones 3 yds apart in a line, then the path turns 90° with two more cones 3 yds apart. One ball, take turns.

How it works: Dribble to the corner cone, then use the inside or outside of the foot to cut sharply around it and accelerate down the new line. Focus on a clean change of direction, then a burst out of the turn. Run it 4–5 times each, trying both cutting feet.

  • Slow into the cut, fast out of it.
  • Chop the ball with a firm touch; don't let it drift wide.
  • First touch after the turn goes into space, not into your feet.
Make it fun: Time each run with a phone stopwatch and post a "family record." Beating your own best (not each other) is the win.
0:25–0:33 · Speed & Agility

Speed-Stick + Trapezoid Combo Course

Setup: Lay 5 speed sticks 1.5 ft apart for quick feet, then 3 trapezoid hurdles ~1 yd apart, then two cones marking a 10 yd sprint finish. No ball for this one.

How it works: Fast feet through the sticks (one step between each), high knees over the hurdles, then explode into a 10 yd sprint through the finish cones. Walk back to recover. 4–5 runs each, alternating so no one waits long.

  • Sticks = quick and light; hurdles = knees up, arms driving.
  • Run tall through the sprint — pump the arms.
  • Full effort for ~6 seconds, then full recovery walking back.
Make it fun: Dad calls out a "power word" (Rocket! Cheetah! Turbo!) right as your son hits the last hurdle — he sprints the finish on the shout.
0:33–0:40 · Speed & Agility

Curved Acceleration Races (with handicap)

Setup: Set a gentle curved lane with 4 cones arcing from a start line to a finish ~15 yds away. Two side-by-side start spots — Dad starts a few yards behind to even things out.

How it works: On "Go!", both race along the curve to the finish. The curve teaches leaning into acceleration and running on the balls of the feet. Best of 5 races. Adjust Dad's handicap so it stays a real contest.

  • First 3 steps are short and punchy — drive the ground away.
  • Lean slightly into the curve, eyes up to the finish.
  • Run through the finish line, don't slow early.
Make it fun: Winner of each race picks Dad's next handicap — hopping starts, backward start, or "sing a line before you run." Keeps every race a new challenge.
0:40–0:48 · Game Application

1v1 King of the Hill

Setup: One small goal made of two cones (2 yds wide) in a ~10×15 yd area. One ball. Dad defends the goal; son attacks.

How it works: Son tries to beat Dad 1v1 and score. Score a goal and you stay "King" and go again; get dispossessed and you swap roles. Use the cuts and close control from the technical block to get past the defender. Play to 5 goals, then Dad defends "for real" but still gives fair chances.

  • Attack the defender's front foot, then cut the other way.
  • Change of pace beats the defender — slow, then explode.
  • Keep the ball on the far foot from the defender.
Make it fun: Every goal gets a named celebration your son invents; Dad has to copy it. Announce the score dramatically like a match commentator.
0:48–0:55 · Game Application

Target Shooting Challenge

Setup: Make a bigger cone goal (4 yds wide) and place a small cone just inside each post as "targets." Shoot from ~8 yds. Dad can play a friendly keeper.

How it works: Each shot: 1 point in the goal, 3 points for hitting a corner target cone. Take a controlling touch, then strike with a locked ankle at the target. 5 shots each per round; first to 15 points wins. Rotate who keeps.

  • Plant foot beside the ball, pointing at the target.
  • Strike with the laces, follow through toward the corner.
  • Placement over power — aim first, then add pace.
Make it fun: Call your shot before you take it ("top corner!"). Calling it AND making it is worth a bonus point and full bragging rights.
0:55–1:00 · Cooldown

Walk, Stretch & Recap

Setup: Drop everything and take a slow walking lap of your space together.

How it works: Easy walk for a minute to bring the heart rate down, then gentle stretches: calves, quads, hamstrings, and a few arm circles — hold each ~15 seconds. While you stretch, do the recap.

  • Breathe slow and deep; no bouncing in the stretches.
  • Sip water throughout.
  • Recap one thing he did great today — name it specifically (that sharp L-cut, a called-corner goal, never quitting a race).
Make it fun: He picks tomorrow's "power word" and you both do the day's best celebration one more time to finish on a high.
