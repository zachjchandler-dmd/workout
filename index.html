Warm-up · 5 min

Number Hunt Dribble

Setup: Each of you with a ball in a small grid about 8 yards square. Scatter 6 cones and give each a number 1–6 (just remember which is which, or scratch a number by each). This is a reaction game — dribble, listen, and go.

How it works: Dribble around the grid with soft little touches. Dad calls a number — "Four!" — and you dribble under control to that cone as fast as you can, do a quick sole-roll or pull-back turn at the cone, then get back to dribbling and wait for the next call. Mix it up: sometimes Dad calls a mini math problem ("three plus two!") so you have to solve it before you sprint. Play about 5 minutes.

Cues: Keep the ball within a step of you (little touches, not big kicks), head up so you can hear and see the cones, and push off hard for those first two steps when a number is called.

🎉 Make it fun: Take turns being the caller, and race it — whoever reaches the right cone and turns first wins that round. Sneaky callers can fake it ("Fiiii… TWO!"). Winner gets to invent the next silly turn move. 😄

A reaction dribbling warm-up: soft touches around a grid, then a quick sprint-and-turn to whatever number Dad calls. Wakes up the feet, sharpens close control, and sneaks in a little quick-thinking (and math!).

Warm-up · 5 min

Spider-Ninja Warm-Up

Setup: No ball. Mark a lane about 12 yards with a cone at each end. This is a themed dynamic-mobility flow — every move is a "Spider-Ninja" skill that loosens the hips, shoulders, and ankles for the soccer work ahead.

How it works: Work down the lane one move at a time, then walk back and start the next: Spider-Man lunges (crawl-lunge, elbow toward your front foot) → ninja side-shuffles → karate straight-leg marches (kick toward the opposite hand) → inchworm crawls → ninja skips (tall, springy) → carioca (grapevine side-step). One length each, about 5 minutes total.

Cues: Move with control, not a rush — reach a little farther in each lunge, stay tall and light on the skips, and keep your belly tight so you feel long and strong. Smooth beats fast here.

🎉 Make it fun: Give every move a superhero sound effect and a "power level." Dad copies you, then you copy Dad — whoever's move looks the coolest earns the "Sensei" title for that round. 🥷🕷️

A no-ball dynamic warm-up dressed up as ninja and spider moves — lunges, shuffles, straight-leg marches, inchworms, skips and carioca. Opens the hips and shoulders and gets the body ready to turn, sprint and shoot.

Technical · ~7 min

Cruyff Turn Gates

Setup: Make 3 little cone "gates" (two cones about a step apart) spread out in a zig-zag, roughly 5 yards between gates. One ball. The Cruyff turn is a famous, sneaky change-of-direction move — you fake a pass or shot, then drag the ball back behind your standing leg and go the other way.

How it works: Dribble up to a gate, plant as if you're about to pass through it, then drag the ball behind your standing leg with the inside of your foot and accelerate away in the new direction toward the next gate. Do a Cruyff turn at each gate. Count how many clean Cruyff turns you can do in 60 seconds and log it — that's a personal record to beat.

Cues: Sell the fake with your body (look like you'll pass), chop the ball with the inside of your foot behind the plant leg, and take a quick burst out of the turn so you'd leave a defender behind.

🎉 Make it fun: Add a "defender" — Dad stands loosely at a gate as a soft obstacle you have to Cruyff around. Every clean turn that beats Dad is a bonus point. Beat your own 60-second count and you pick the next drill's theme! ⚽

Cruyff turns through cone gates: fake, drag the ball back behind the standing leg, and accelerate away. Builds a real change-of-direction weapon plus close control under a little time pressure.

Technical · ~7 min

Wall Target Passing Ladder

Setup: Stand about 4–5 yards from the rebounding wall with one ball. Pick (or chalk) 3 target spots on the wall — say a low-left, a middle, and a low-right. This drill trains passing accuracy and the first touch that comes right back at you.

How it works: Pass at target 1, control the rebound with a clean first touch, then pass at target 2, control, then target 3 — that's one "ladder." Keep climbing the ladder 1→2→3→1→2→3. Count how many clean target-hits-with-control you get in 60 seconds and log it as your record. A hit only counts if you both strike the target zone and settle the rebound under control.

Cues: Lock your ankle and pass with the big inside-foot surface for accuracy, open your body so you can see the next target, and cushion the rebound — soft first touch, out of your feet, ready to pass again.

🎉 Make it fun: Name the targets ("top-bin!", "bottom corner!") and call your shot before you hit it, like calling pocket in pool. Call it and hit it = double points. Dad rebounds a couple to make it a rally. 🎯

Pass at a target, control the rebound, repeat up the ladder of three targets. Sharpens passing accuracy and a clean, ready first touch — two of the most useful skills in the whole game.

Speed & Agility · ~15 min

Slalom Sprint Circuit

Setup: Build a 4-station course in a line: (1) speed sticks laid as a ladder, then (2) two or three trapezoid hurdles, then (3) five cones in a zig-zag "slalom," then (4) a finish cone about 12 yards past the last slalom cone. No ball — this is pure quick feet and speed.

How it works: Go through the whole course as fast as you can with control: quick "in-in-out-out" feet through the ladder → two-footed hops over the hurdles → weave through the zig-zag cones → explode in a straight sprint through the finish cone. Time the full circuit and log your fastest run — lower is better, so that becomes a record to chase. Full rest (walk back slowly) between attempts; do 4–6 runs.

Cues: Fast feet but light and under control through the ladder and hurdles, lean and push off the outside foot to change direction in the slalom, and then run tall and hard all the way through the finish cone — don't slow up early.

🎉 Make it fun: Dad runs it too and you compare times — with a fair handicap so it's a photo finish. Add a "commentator" calling the race, and give each run a nickname ("the Rocket Run!"). Beat your best time and ring an imaginary bell. 🏁

A timed four-station course — ladder quick-feet, hurdle hops, a cone slalom, then a straight sprint finish. Trains foot speed, change of direction and acceleration, all the athletic stuff that makes a soccer player dangerous.

Game · ~15 min

Finishing School Shootout

Setup: A start cone about 15 yards from a goal (a real goal or two cones). Put one "defender cone" halfway as a gate to beat. Dad plays keeper. One ball. This is where the turns and touches from today turn into goals.

How it works: From the start cone, dribble in, beat the defender cone with a move (a Cruyff turn from earlier counts!), then finish past Dad-in-goal. Each of you takes 10 attempts. A goal in the corners (past a stretched keeper) is worth 2 points; a straight-on goal is 1. Trade turns, keep score, and log your total points out of 10.

Cues: Take a positive touch around the defender cone (don't slow down), pick your spot early and place it (side-foot for accuracy over power), and follow your shot in case of a rebound.

🎉 Make it fun: Big commentary and celebrations — name each finish ("the Rainbow Rocket!") and do a goal celebration you invent together. Dad the keeper can be gloriously dramatic. Last shot is always a "golden goal" worth double for a big finish. ⚽🎉

Dribble in, beat a defender, and finish past the keeper — 10 shots each, corners worth double. Turns the day's skills into real goal-scoring, with a scoreboard and big celebrations to end on a high.

Cooldown · ~5 min

Walk, Stretch & Recap

Walk it off: Easy 2–3 minute walk together until your breathing settles back down. Keep sipping water — it's heading for a hot one.

Stretch: Give the legs some love — calves, quads, hamstrings, hip flexors, and glutes. Hold each about 20 seconds, relaxed, no bouncing.

🌧️ Rain Backup (small-space, ~60 min)

Storm rolls in early or you're stuck inside? No field or running room needed — a couple of square feet each is plenty. We keep the ball skills going in place and mix in a bodyweight circuit for the athletic work.

Warm-up (2 rounds of 90 sec): march in place → arm circles & trunk twists → light in-place fast feet. Rest 30 seconds between rounds.

Ball mastery in place (3 rounds, 40 sec each, needs only a couple square feet): toe taps on top of the ball → side-to-side sole rolls → foundation inside-inside taps → pull-back & push. Count your best 30-second toe-tap total if you want a number for the record book.

Strength + quick-feet circuit (3 rounds, 40 sec on / 20 sec off), all in place: squats → push-ups (knees OK) → mountain climbers → plank hold → in-place fast feet. Rest a minute between rounds.

Finish: The normal stretch — calves, quads, hamstrings, hip flexors, glutes, ~20 seconds each — then trade one "proud moment" and a high-five.

🎉 Make it fun: Reaction round — Dad claps and you switch instantly (one clap = fast feet, two claps = freeze in a plank). Miss a call and you owe five toe-taps. 😄
